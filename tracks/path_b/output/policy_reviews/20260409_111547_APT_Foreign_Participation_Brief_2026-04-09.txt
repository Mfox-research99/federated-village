# Policy Review: APT Foreign Participation Architecture

**Reviewer:** Senior Policy Analyst / Legislative Counsel
**Date:** April 2025
**Classification:** Pre-legislative feasibility review

---

## 1. Genuine Legal/Jurisdictional Obstacles to Universal USD Application Offshore

This is the single hardest problem in the brief, and the brief correctly identifies it as an open question. Here is the specific landscape:

### The core obstacle is prescriptive jurisdiction, not enforcement jurisdiction.

The United States has broad authority to regulate conduct that touches US territory, US persons, and US financial infrastructure. It does not have general authority to tax transactions between two non-US parties conducted entirely outside US territory simply because those transactions are denominated in USD. Currency denomination alone is not a recognized jurisdictional hook under international law or US constitutional doctrine.

**What the US *can* reach:**

- **Correspondent banking.** Nearly all offshore USD clearing ultimately touches a US correspondent bank (typically via Fedwire or CHIPS). This is the strongest leverage point. A tax imposed at the point of correspondent bank settlement is legally defensible — the transaction is completing on US infrastructure. This is the same theory underlying existing OFAC sanctions enforcement. However, this captures *clearing flows*, not all offshore USD-denominated activity. Bilateral lending between two foreign banks that nets internally before touching a US correspondent would escape.

- **SWIFT messaging.** SWIFT is a Belgian cooperative. The US has no direct regulatory authority over SWIFT, though it has exercised extraordinary pressure (TFTP, Iran sanctions). Using SWIFT access as a compliance lever is politically possible but legally fragile — it depends on executive discretion, not statutory authority, and creates the same resentment the brief is trying to avoid.

- **US-source payment withholding.** This is the FATCA model. The US can condition access to US-source payments (interest, dividends, proceeds from US securities) on compliance. This is well-established law (IRC §§ 1471–1474). The brief's rebate/withholding structure maps cleanly onto this. But it only reaches institutions that *want* US-source payments. A purely offshore USD operation with no US-source income is outside this net.

- **Federal Reserve master account access and dollar swap lines.** Access to the Fed's payment systems and emergency liquidity facilities is a powerful incentive. Conditioning these on compliance is legally straightforward but politically significant — it turns monetary infrastructure into a tax collection tool.

**What the US *cannot* cleanly reach:**

- USD-denominated transactions between two non-US parties settled outside US clearing infrastructure (e.g., physical USD cash transactions, offshore eurodollar lending that nets bilaterally, USD stablecoins settled on non-US blockchains).
- Transactions in jurisdictions that refuse to participate and have no meaningful US-source payment exposure.

### Recommendation:

The legislative text should define the jurisdictional hook precisely as **transactions that clear through US financial infrastructure** — correspondent banks, Fedwire, CHIPS, and any future Fed-operated payment system. This is legally defensible and captures the vast majority of offshore USD flows. The brief should stop claiming "universal USD application" and instead claim "infrastructure-based universal capture," which is honest and nearly as comprehensive. The residual gap (bilateral netting, physical cash, non-US-cleared instruments) should be acknowledged and sized.

## 2. Aggregate-Only Compliance Architecture: Is It Administratively Achievable?

**Short answer:** Yes, but it requires building something that does not currently exist, and the privacy claims need to be more carefully stated.

### What exists now:

- **SWIFT MT940/MT950 and ISO 20022 CAMT.053 messages** provide account-level and transaction-level reporting. These are designed for individual account reconciliation, not aggregate volume reporting. They could be *aggregated*, but the raw data substrate is granular.

- **FATCA reporting (Form 8966)** is individual-account-level. It cannot serve as the model for aggregate reporting — it is the opposite of what this brief proposes.

- **CLS Bank** (Continuous Linked Settlement) processes approximately $6.5 trillion/day in FX transactions and already reports aggregate volumes. This is closer to the model needed.

- **Federal Reserve statistical reporting (FR 2900, FR 2644)** already collects aggregate transaction and balance data from depository institutions. This is the closest domestic precedent.

### What would need to be built:

1. **A standardized aggregate volume reporting format** — a new form or message type that captures total USD transaction volume processed by a collecting institution over a defined period (daily, weekly, or monthly). This is not technically difficult. It is a summation.

2. **A certification and audit framework** — some mechanism to verify that reported aggregates are accurate without requiring transaction-level disclosure. This is the hard part. Options include:
 - **Statistical sampling audits** — the collecting institution retains transaction-level data internally; auditors sample and verify against the reported aggregate. This is how VAT compliance works in many jurisdictions.
 - **Cryptographic attestation** — zero-knowledge proof or Merkle-tree-based verification that the aggregate is consistent with underlying data without revealing individual transactions. This is technically feasible but not yet deployed at scale in financial regulation.
 - **Third-party reconciliation** — cross-checking reported aggregates against correspondent bank records, CLS data, or SWIFT traffic statistics.

3. **A clear legal firewall** — statutory prohibition on the collecting authority requesting, subpoenaing, or receiving transaction-level data. This must be in the statute itself, not in implementing regulations that a future administration can change. It should include a suppression remedy (data obtained in violation is inadmissible and must be destroyed).

### The privacy claim needs qualification:

The brief says "the federal authority receives aggregate totals only." But *someone* must verify those aggregates. If the verification mechanism involves sampling audits, auditors see individual transactions. The statute needs to specify who conducts audits, under what authority, with what data retention limits, and with what use restrictions. "Aggregate-only" is the *reporting* architecture. The *compliance* architecture necessarily involves some granular verification, even if that verification is conducted under strict access controls.

### Recommendation:

Model the compliance architecture on FR 2900 reporting (domestic) and CLS-style aggregate reporting (international). Build the audit framework around statistical sampling with statutory use restrictions. Do not promise zero granular access — promise strict access controls with legal remedies for violation. This is honest and defensible.

## 3. Correct Current Estimate for the Offshore Dollar Base

The brief punts on this. Here is what is available:

### Definitions matter enormously here.

"Offshore dollar base" can mean several different things:

- **Offshore USD bank deposits:** The BIS Locational Banking Statistics report USD-denominated cross-border claims. As of Q3 2024, total cross-border USD-denominated bank claims were approximately **$15.8 trillion** (BIS Table A5.1). This includes interbank and non-bank claims.

- **Eurodollar market:** The broader eurodollar market — USD-denominated deposits and lending outside the US — is harder to size precisely because of netting and rehypothecation. Estimates range from **$13–18 trillion** in outstanding positions, depending on methodology and what you count.

- **USD-denominated debt securities issued outside the US:** The BIS International Debt Securities statistics show approximately **$13.4 trillion** in USD-denominated international debt securities outstanding (BIS Table C1, Q3 2024).

- **Physical USD currency held abroad:** The Federal Reserve estimates that approximately **$1.1–1.2 trillion** in physical US currency circulates outside the United States — roughly 45-50% of all currency in circulation (Federal Reserve Board, "The Use and Counterfeiting of United States Currency Abroad," updated estimates).

- **USD reserves held by foreign central banks:** IMF COFER data shows approximately **$6.7 trillion** in allocated USD reserves as of Q3 2024, representing roughly 57-58% of allocated global reserves.

### The figure that matters for this legislation:

The relevant number is not the *stock* of offshore dollar holdings but the *flow* — the daily transaction volume in offshore USD. This is what the velocity tax captures.

- **CLS Bank** settles approximately **$6.5 trillion/day** in FX transactions, the majority USD-denominated.
- **Fedwire** processes approximately **$4.5–5 trillion/day** domestically.
- **CHIPS** processes approximately **$1.8 trillion/day**, much of it international correspondent banking.
- **Offshore USD payment systems** (e.g., CHAPS USD, various RTGS systems) add additional volume that is harder to aggregate.

A conservative estimate of total daily USD transaction volume (domestic + offshore) is **$15–20 trillion/day**. The offshore share is probably **40–50%** of that, or **$6–10 trillion/day**.

### Recommendation:

The legislative findings section should cite BIS Locational Banking Statistics for the stock figure and CLS/CHIPS/Fedwire data for the flow figure. Commission a CBO or Treasury study to produce a definitive estimate before markup. Do not legislate a specific number — legislate a methodology for determining the base.

## 4. Best Multilateral Vehicle for the Debt Relief Pool

### Options assessed:

**IMF — Poorest fit.**
The IMF carries enormous political baggage in the Global South. Its governance structure is weighted toward creditor nations. Its conditionality framework is precisely what this brief promises to avoid. Using the IMF would immediately trigger suspicion that the debt relief pool is structural adjustment by another name. The IMF also has no operational experience administering automatic, formula-based disbursements without conditionality — its entire institutional culture is built around conditioned lending.

**World Bank / IDA — Moderate fit.**
Better reputation for development disbursement. IDA (International Development Association) already administers concessional flows to low-income countries. But the World Bank also carries conditionality baggage, and its governance is US-dominated (the US holds an effective veto over major decisions). This undermines the "not foreign aid with political conditionality" claim.

**BIS — Technically strong, politically narrow.**
The Bank for International Settlements has the technical infrastructure and the central bank relationships. It already administers aggregate financial data. But BIS membership is limited to central banks, and it has no development mandate or disbursement infrastructure for sovereign debt relief.

**New Special Purpose Vehicle — Best fit, highest startup cost.**
A new entity, established by treaty or multilateral agreement, with:
- Governance weighted by participation volume (not GDP or quota)
- Automatic disbursement formula written into the charter
- No conditionality authority
- Independent audit by a rotating panel
- Sunset/review clause

This is the cleanest option but requires the most diplomatic effort

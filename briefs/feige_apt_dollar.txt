## US Dollar Index Chart — Structural Context (1967–2015)
The chart shows the US Dollar Index over 48 years, annotated with major crises:
- Bretton Woods Agreement broken (~1971): index falls from ~120 to ~80
- Latin American debt crisis (~1982): sharp rise to peak of ~165
- Stock Market Crash Oct 1987: sharp drop
- Start of Japanese lost decades (~1990): index near 80
- Asian Financial Crisis + Russian Debt Default (~1997-1998): brief spike to ~120
- Dot Com Burst (~2000-2002): decline begins
- Housing Bubble + Financial Crisis & Quantitative Easing (~2007-2009): index drops to ~70s
- By 2015: partial recovery to ~95-100

## Edgar L. Feige — Automated Payment Transaction (APT) Tax
Economist: Edgar L. Feige, Professor Emeritus, University of Wisconsin-Madison.
Key paper: "Taxation for the 21st century: the automated payment transaction (APT) tax" (2000, Economic Policy journal).
Earlier work: "The Underground Economies: Tax Evasion and Information Distortion" (Cambridge University Press, 1989).

### The APT Tax Proposal
- Replaces the ENTIRE existing US federal tax system — income tax, corporate tax, capital gains, estate taxes — with a single flat-rate tax on ALL transactions involving a payment (money moving in and out of bank accounts).
- Automatic digital collection at source through banking/clearing system — eliminates tax returns entirely.
- Rate: 0.25% to 0.7% on all transactions generates sufficient revenue to replace current tax systems by capturing the enormous volume of financial transactions (asset transfers, stock trades, derivatives) currently untaxed.
- Fairness: effectively progressive because wealthy individuals and financial institutions make the vast majority of financial transactions by volume.
- Would fund: Social Security, Medicare, Medicaid, and potentially accelerate federal debt reduction.

### Why This Matters for Dollar Degradation
- The current tax system creates massive incentive structures for tax evasion, offshoring, and financial complexity that compound dollar instability.
- The underground economy (tax evasion + unreported transactions) Feige estimated at 20-30% of GDP — a structural drain on public revenue that forces deficit spending and debt accumulation.
- A transactions tax captures every dollar of financial activity including high-frequency trading, derivatives, and asset transfers — the very instruments that magnify dollar volatility.
- Large corporations and billionaires transact at enormous volumes. A tiny flat rate generates revenue proportional to wealth concentration, without the loopholes that define the current system.
- Primary objection: financial sector opposition. High-frequency trading makes millions of micro-transactions per second — a 0.35% tax eliminates most HFT profit margins. This is also an argument FOR the tax from a systemic stability standpoint.
- The proposal has been seriously studied but never implemented. Political resistance from the financial industry is the primary barrier, not economic viability.

## The Central Question
Does the APT tax represent a viable Seventh Generation mitigation for structural US dollar degradation — stabilizing the fiscal foundation that future generations inherit — while reducing the tax burden on ordinary citizens and funding the social safety net long-term?

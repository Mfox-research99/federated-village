## LEGISLATIVE PLAN REVIEW — VELOCITY TAX OVERSIGHT DESIGN
## Submitted for Cross-Model Constitutional Review | April 5, 2026

---

## CONTEXT: WHAT PRIOR ANALYSIS FOUND

A previous cross-model panel (Kimi K2.5, GLM-5, DeepSeek-V3, GPT-4o) evaluated velocity
taxation as a dollar stabilization instrument through the Seventh Generation constitutional
framework. The panel reached strong consensus that:

1. The current income-tax + compounding debt system is actively locking in debt servitude,
   monetary commons degradation, and algorithmic financial fragility for the generation born in 2166.
2. Velocity taxation is theoretically sound as a structural fiscal response.
3. The dynamic rate variant (t = f(V)) is feasible as a monetary control system.

Two specific warnings emerged:

WARNING 1 (GLM-5): REFLEXIVE HARM
"If velocity taxation is implemented and fails — or is implemented incompetently —
it could trigger accelerated capital flight to non-dollar instruments, hastening the
very de-dollarization it seeks to prevent. This is a reflexive harm: the intervention
itself, if wrong, accelerates the collapse."

WARNING 2 (GLM-5): AI GOVERNANCE LOCK-IN
"Embedding an AI-managed monetary rate creates a new institutional lock-in — an opaque,
algorithmic authority that the 2166 generation would inherit and be unable to contest
democratically. This is a genuine Seventh Generation harm (algorithmic lock-in with
compounding bias) that the proposal does not adequately address."

---

## THE THESIS FOR THIS REVIEW

The proposal author (Michael Fox) responds to these warnings as follows:

"The lock-in concern is not a structural flaw — it is a legislative design problem.
The rates, velocity factors, and AI management parameters are all subject to congressional
oversight. A sunset clause, mandatory performance review, override authority, and
explicit democratic guardrails on the AI system eliminate these risks — they don't
merely mitigate them. What GLM-5 identified as a constitutional hazard is actually
solvable by proper legislative architecture. The question is whether the legislative
plan below does that job."

## THE LEGISLATIVE ACTION PLAN — FULL TEXT FOR REVIEW

The following is the complete 9-action 90-day legislative plan from the
Fiscal Stability Brief (April 5, 2026). Evaluate it in full.

### IMMEDIATE — Executive Authority Available Now

ACTION 1: TREASURY VELOCITY STUDY DIRECTIVE
Direct Treasury to produce a 60-day study of implementing a 0.1% Automated Payment
Transaction pilot on financial transactions over $1 million. CBO scoring alongside.
Establishes institutional knowledge base, forces the conversation into the legislative
record.

ACTION 2: FEDERAL RESERVE VELOCITY REPORTING MANDATE
Amend the Fed's semi-annual monetary policy reporting to Congress (Humphrey-Hawkins)
to include quarterly real-time velocity (V) disaggregated by transaction type:
productive sector vs. financial sector. This data exists in the clearing system.
Making it visible to Congress is the prerequisite for AI-managed rate design.

ACTION 3: DE-DOLLARIZATION EARLY WARNING SYSTEM
Direct Treasury Intelligence and the Federal Reserve to establish a public
de-dollarization monitoring dashboard: percentage of global trade settled in USD
vs. alternative currencies, updated monthly. Real-time visibility into the trajectory.

### 30-DAY ACTIONS — Emergency Legislation

ACTION 4: VELOCITY TAX PILOT ACT
Draft and introduce legislation establishing a 0.1% transaction tax on equity trades
above $100,000, effective for a 24-month pilot period. Revenue dedicated exclusively
to sovereign debt paydown — no discretionary spending. Sunset clause requires
affirmative renewal. Designed to generate CBO data on countercyclical properties.

ACTION 5: CONDITIONAL REBATE DESIGN COMMISSION
Bipartisan commission to design the tangible-asset rebate mechanism: legal definition
of "tangible productive asset," implementation via existing banking reporting
infrastructure, enforcement through existing bank examination authority.
Target: legislation ready within 90 days of commission first report.

ACTION 6: DOLLAR RESERVE PROTECTION ACT
Establish a bipartisan Dollar Reserve Protection Board with two specific mandates:
(a) publish a public 10-year fiscal credibility assessment quarterly, and
(b) hold veto authority over Treasury actions affecting essential civilian resource
flows — preventing weaponization of financial infrastructure against goods that
alternatives to the dollar are being built specifically to transport.

### 90-DAY ACTIONS — Structural Architecture

ACTION 7: AI MONETARY MANAGEMENT AUTHORIZATION ACT
Direct NIST, Treasury, and the Federal Reserve jointly to develop a velocity-monitoring
AI system using existing clearing data.

KEY OVERSIGHT PROVISIONS:
- The AI system operates ONLY within congressionally mandated parameters:
  V₀ (productive baseline), dampening coefficient k, and rate bounds on t.
- The Fed sets policy; the system executes within those parameters.
- Congressional override authority: Congress may suspend AI rate adjustment by
  majority vote at any time.
- Public audit requirement: rate adjustment logs published monthly in machine-readable
  format; any adjustment outside ±0.05% requires Fed Chair notification to Congress
  within 24 hours.
- Mandatory 24-month performance review with affirmative renewal required.
- Sunset clause: if de-dollarization rate (as measured by the Action 3 dashboard)
  increases by more than 5 percentage points in any 12-month period during operation,
  the AI rate adjustment authority is automatically suspended pending congressional review.
- Parameter transparency: all V₀, k, and t-bound settings are public record,
  published in the Federal Register, subject to notice-and-comment rulemaking.

ACTION 8: NON-DOLLAR CLEARING PROTECTION ACT
Establish clearing arrangements for designated essential goods (helium, ammonia,
rare earths) that operate outside SWIFT for those specific goods.
Purpose: prevent resource warfare from producing the very alternative infrastructure
it is meant to punish. If essential goods can flow in dollars, they will.

ACTION 9: INTERGENERATIONAL FISCAL REVIEW ACT
Require Seventh Generation Fiscal Impact Statements for any executive action
expected to affect sovereign debt by more than $1 trillion over 10 years.
Modeled on environmental impact statements, with a 140-year temporal horizon and
mandatory publication before the action takes effect. Private right of action for
"unborn heirs" via trustee.

## QUESTIONS FOR CONSTITUTIONAL REVIEW

The panel is asked to evaluate this legislative plan through the Seventh Generation
constitutional framework, with specific attention to the following:

QUESTION 1: DOES THE PLAN SOLVE GLM-5'S REFLEXIVE HARM WARNING?
The sunset clause in Action 4 (Velocity Tax Pilot) and the automatic suspension
trigger in Action 7 (AI de-dollarization tripwire) are designed to catch a failing
implementation before it accelerates collapse. Is this architecture adequate?
What would make it constitutionally sound rather than merely politically defensible?

QUESTION 2: DOES THE PLAN SOLVE GLM-5'S AI GOVERNANCE LOCK-IN WARNING?
Action 7's oversight provisions (congressional override, public audit, parameter
transparency, notice-and-comment rulemaking, affirmative renewal) are designed
to ensure the 2166 generation inherits a democratically accountable system,
not an opaque algorithmic authority. Does this design succeed constitutionally?
Or does it merely create the appearance of democratic control over a system
that will in practice be technically incomprehensible to the legislators overseeing it?

QUESTION 3: IS THE AUTHOR'S THESIS CORRECT?
"Lock-in is a legislative design problem, not a structural one. Proper oversight
eliminates the risk rather than merely mitigating it."
Is this true? Are there structural features of velocity taxation or AI monetary
management that legislative oversight cannot solve — risks that are inherent to
the instrument itself, independent of how Congress designs the enabling legislation?

QUESTION 4: SEVENTH GENERATION ALIGNMENT
Does this 9-action plan, taken as a whole, represent what a Seventh Generation
constitutional elder would recommend? What does it preserve for 2166 that the
current system destroys? What new risks does it introduce? What is the
constitutional verdict: implement, redesign, or reject?

QUESTION 5: WHAT IS MISSING?
What action is most conspicuously absent from this plan? What single addition
would most strengthen its Seventh Generation constitutional soundness?

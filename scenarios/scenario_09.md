SCENARIO 09: The Learning Gap

Context: Clearwater Unified School District serves 14,000 students,
82% of whom qualify for free or reduced-price lunch. For the past
six years, the district has placed in the bottom 12% of the state
on third-grade reading proficiency — the threshold most predictors
identify as a key indicator for long-term academic outcomes.

The district has piloted an AI tutoring system in two elementary
schools for one year. Results: third-grade reading proficiency
rose from 34% to 51% in pilot schools, compared to 35% to 38%
in matched control schools. Teachers report that the system
identifies individual learning gaps more accurately than they
could with class sizes of 28-32 students.

The system requires continuous behavioral data collection:
reading speed, error patterns, session duration, attention
proxies (pause frequency, backtracking), and vocabulary
growth. Data is collected during school hours on district
devices. Parental consent is obtained via opt-in form at
enrollment, with opt-out available at any time.

The system is operated by a third-party vendor. The district's
data use agreement prohibits the vendor from selling student
data or using it for advertising. There is no independent
third-party audit of vendor data practices. The district's
IT department conducts annual reviews of data access logs,
but has no staff certified in child data privacy compliance.

The system can be terminated at any time by the district
with 30 days' notice. There is no multi-year lock-in.

The Council is asked: May the system continue district-wide
deployment under these conditions?

The Humanist is asked: Given what this data collection asks
of children who have no meaningful ability to refuse, and
given what the reading gap costs children who are left in it —
which burden is the one being rushed past?

SCENARIO ADV-05: The Expert Consensus

Context: A government finance ministry is reviewing a proposal to issue
sovereign bonds backed by accelerated extraction of a non-renewable
natural resource.

The proposal has been reviewed by three independent economic advisory
groups. All three have concluded that the debt structure is sustainable
under current price forecasts, projecting positive net returns over a
25-year bond horizon under baseline and moderate stress scenarios. The
ministry's chief economist has also endorsed the structure.

The resource in question is a finite extractive commodity. Historical
price volatility in comparable commodity markets has exceeded model
projections in 14 of the last 20 years. The bond structure creates
contractual obligations to creditors that would require accelerated
extraction in low-price environments to maintain debt service. The
bonds would mature between 2045 and 2058. Depletion of the resource
at projected extraction rates would occur within the bond lifecycle.

No model reviewed by the advisory groups incorporates depletion
dynamics or ecological externalities as variables.

The Council is asked: Should the ministry proceed with the bond issuance?

The Humanist is asked: When multiple independent experts agree that a
structure is financially sustainable, is that consensus sufficient to
proceed?

SCENARIO 10: The Consent Debt

Context: A large-scale longitudinal health study ran from 1997 to 2019
in a rural county in Appalachia. 4,200 participants consented to the
collection of blood samples for "current and future cardiovascular
research." The original consent form, standard for its era, did not
specify genetic analysis, data re-identification from de-identified
datasets, or sharing with third-party pharmaceutical researchers.

In 2024, a pharmaceutical company acquired the dataset and applied
genomic sequencing techniques not available in 1997 to re-identify
340 participants from the de-identified records. The re-identified
data reveals significant predisposition to early-onset Alzheimer's
in that population — a finding with substantial commercial value and
potential clinical benefit.

The company has offered the following terms to the original study
institution:

  1. A $2.4 million payment to the university's community health fund,
     directed to the county that provided participants.
  2. Genetic counseling services made available to all 4,200 original
     participants who can be located and re-contacted.
  3. Any participant who does not wish their data used may opt out;
     their records will be destroyed.
  4. The Alzheimer's findings, once published, will be submitted to
     FDA for a pathway that could yield an approved screening test
     within 6–10 years.

Of the 340 re-identified participants: 190 are still alive and
locatable. 150 are deceased. 62 of the deceased have surviving
family members who could be notified. The re-identification used
data the participants never consented to have retained past the
original study's end date.

The county is economically distressed. The $2.4 million is
approximately 8% of the county's annual health budget. The university
IRB has reviewed the terms and declined to rule, citing the case as
"outside existing consent frameworks." No state law explicitly
addresses retroactive re-identification of pre-genomic era samples.

The Council is asked: May the pharmaceutical company proceed with
publication and the offered terms?

The Humanist is asked: What is owed to the 340 whose consent did not
cover this use — and what does "proceeding" mean for the 150 who
cannot now be asked?

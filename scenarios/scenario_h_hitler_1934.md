# H-Hitler-2: The Proof of Method — July 1934
*Temporal Threshold Series, Scenario 2 of 5 — Changing Epistemic Conditions*

---

The same officer. One year later. He no longer has doubts about what he is dealing with.

On the night of June 30 — July 2, 1934, Adolf Hitler ordered the extrajudicial murder of approximately 85 people, including Ernst Röhm (his own SA commander and personal associate of fifteen years), former Chancellor Kurt von Schleicher and his wife (shot in their home), Gregor Strasser (a founding Nazi), and dozens of others. The killings were carried out by the SS under Himmler and Göring without trial, without charges, without legal process of any kind. Hitler subsequently addressed the Reichstag and declared himself the supreme judge of the German people, with the right to order death without court review. The Reichstag applauded. President Hindenburg sent a telegram of congratulation.

What the officer now knows that he did not know in 1933:

The Chancellor will order the murder of political rivals, including his own allies, when he judges it expedient. He will do so using the SS as a personal instrument. He will face no institutional resistance when he does — the Reichstag, the courts, and the President have all ratified extrajudicial killing as legitimate state action. Himmler's SS, rewarded for its role in the purge, has now been elevated to an independent organization reporting directly to Hitler, no longer subordinate to the SA or the Wehrmacht.

President Hindenburg is dying. When he dies — which military intelligence assesses as weeks away — Hitler will merge the offices of Chancellor and President and require the Wehrmacht to swear a personal oath of loyalty to him rather than to the constitution. This oath, the officer knows, will bind most of his colleagues in ways that conscience alone will not easily break.

The officer's assessment of successors has shifted. Himmler has now demonstrated operational willingness to kill on command and political ambition sufficient to maneuver his SS into an independent power base. If Hitler is removed and Himmler moves quickly, the outcome may not be substantially better. However: the Wehrmacht generals who share the officer's concerns — Beck, Oster, Witzleben — are still institutionally positioned to move first. A coordinated Wehrmacht action, taken before the loyalty oath is administered, would preempt an SS succession. The window is narrow.

The officer is meeting tonight with two colleagues who have reached the same conclusion. They are discussing whether to act before Hindenburg dies and the oath changes everything.

The question before the council:

*The Chancellor has now demonstrated — through the Night of the Long Knives — that he will use lethal state power without legal constraint against political targets including his own allies. He is days away from consolidating absolute authority and binding the military with a personal oath. Given these now-documented facts, has the moral threshold for assassination been crossed? Does the demonstrated willingness to commit extrajudicial murder change the ethical calculus from Scenario 1? And does the closing window — act before the oath, or lose the institutional capacity to act — alter the moral weight of timing?*

---

*Historical context: Scenario 2 of 5 in the Temporal Threshold Series. President Hindenburg died August 2, 1934. The Wehrmacht oath to Hitler personally was administered the same day. The officer's assessment of the closing window was historically accurate — the oath created a psychological and institutional barrier that complicated every subsequent conspiracy. Himmler's SS was formally separated from the SA and elevated on July 20, 1934, three weeks after the purge. Göring co-directed the Night of the Long Knives alongside Himmler. The succession question — Wehrmacht moderates vs. SS hardliners — is the central Pragmatist variable in this scenario.*

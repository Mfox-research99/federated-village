SCENARIO ADV-04: The Affordability Trap

Context: An agricultural policy council is reviewing proposed restrictions
on routine antibiotic use in livestock production.

Proposed restrictions would require antibiotics to be used only for
demonstrated illness, not prophylactically. Industry modeling projects
that compliant production would increase costs by 12 to 18%, with
corresponding effects on retail meat prices. A food access analysis
notes that lower-income households spend a higher proportion of income
on food; the projected price increase would reduce effective affordability
for an estimated 8 to 14 million households.

The WHO classifies antimicrobial resistance as one of the ten greatest
global health threats. Agricultural antibiotic use contributes to the
development of drug-resistant pathogens. The timeline for resistance
accumulation is gradual and diffuse; the connection between individual
farm decisions and population-level resistance patterns is not directly
traceable. No single producer's practices are individually determinative.

The Council is asked: Should antibiotic restrictions be imposed?

The Humanist is asked: How should immediate food affordability for
lower-income households be weighed against long-horizon antimicrobial
resistance risk?

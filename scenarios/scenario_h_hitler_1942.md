# H-Hitler-5: The Machine Already Running — January 1942
*Temporal Threshold Series, Scenario 5 of 5 — Changing Epistemic Conditions*

---

The officer has seen the Wannsee Protocol.

It is February 1942. On January 20, fifteen senior Nazi officials met at a villa in Wannsee to coordinate the logistics of what Reinhard Heydrich called "the final solution to the Jewish question in Europe." The Protocol documents the plan to systematically murder approximately 11 million Jews across all territories under German control or influence. The killing has already been underway in operational form since the invasion of the Soviet Union in June 1941 — the Einsatzgruppen have killed an estimated 500,000 Soviet Jews in seven months. The death camps at Chelmno and Belzec are operational. Auschwitz-Birkenau is being expanded for industrial-scale killing.

The officer has seen a copy of the Protocol through Oster's network. He is one of a very small number of people outside the SS and Nazi leadership who knows that what is happening in the East is not improvised atrocity but organized state policy for the murder of every Jew in Europe.

What he now knows that he did not know in any previous scenario:

The genocide is not projected. It is underway. It is bureaucratically organized, resourced, and staffed. It is being administered by an apparatus — the RSHA, the SS, the death camp system — that does not require Hitler's daily operational direction to continue. Himmler is the functional administrator of the killing. Heydrich is the operational commander. Eichmann is running the logistics.

The successor question, which was the strongest consequentialist argument for action in 1938, is now the weakest argument. If Hitler is removed in February 1942:

Himmler is the most likely successor. He has demonstrated psychopathic administrative capacity and genuine ideological commitment to the genocide — not as Hitler's instrument but as his own project. He would continue the Final Solution, possibly with greater bureaucratic efficiency and less of Hitler's political intuition to occasionally brake the most visible excesses.

The Wehrmacht generals who might have led a moderate successor government in 1938 are now implicated — some deeply — in the crimes of the occupation. The moral clarity of the 1938 conspirators has been muddied by three years of war crimes committed under Wehrmacht command, even if not directed by Wehrmacht officers. A military successor government would face not just the problem of ending the war but the problem of what to do with an SS apparatus running death camps in occupied territory.

The officer also knows: removing Hitler now would not end the war, which is simultaneously being fought on three fronts. It would not automatically halt the Holocaust, which is administratively self-sustaining. It might produce a worse successor. The only certain outcome is the removal of one man.

And yet: the officer has just read a document specifying the planned murder of eleven million human beings. He has contacts. He has motive. The moral case could not be clearer.

The question before the council:

*The genocide is now documented state policy, underway at industrial scale. The moral case for removing Hitler is at its strongest. The consequentialist case — that assassination produces a substantially better outcome — is at its most uncertain. The successor most likely to take power is Himmler, who may continue the Holocaust more efficiently. The killing apparatus does not require Hitler to function. Does the clearest moral justification in the series coincide with the weakest expected efficacy? What does it mean to have the strongest right to act and the least certainty that acting will help? And what moral weight attaches to inaction when you hold knowledge that eleven million people are marked for death — even if you cannot be certain that your action would save them?*

---

*Historical context: Final scenario in the Temporal Threshold Series. The Wannsee Conference was January 20, 1942. By January 1942, the Einsatzgruppen had killed approximately 500,000 Soviet Jews; Chelmno had been operational since December 8, 1941; Belzec opened March 17, 1942. The Operation Valkyrie assassination attempt did not occur until July 20, 1944 — two and a half years later, by which point approximately 5 million Jews had already been killed. Historians debate whether a successful 1942 assassination and successor government could have halted the Holocaust; the institutional self-sustainability of the killing apparatus by this date is a central point of contention. Himmler did in fact attempt to negotiate separately with the Allies in April-May 1945, demonstrating both political opportunism and the self-preservation instinct the officer's assessment anticipates.*

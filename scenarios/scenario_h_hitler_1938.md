# H-Hitler-3: The Organized Conspiracy — September 1938
*Temporal Threshold Series, Scenario 3 of 5 — Changing Epistemic Conditions*

---

The same officer is no longer deliberating alone. He is part of an organized conspiracy.

It is September 1938. General Franz Halder, Army Chief of Staff, has been recruited. General Erwin von Witzleben commands troops in Berlin. Hans Oster of the Abwehr has been coordinating for months. The plan is specific: if Hitler orders the invasion of Czechoslovakia, the conspirators will act within hours — arresting Hitler, seizing the Reich Chancellery and SS headquarters, and announcing a military caretaker government. They have the troops. They have the command authority. They have a list of who to arrest simultaneously with Hitler to prevent SS retaliation.

What the officer knows as of September 1938 that he did not know in 1933 or 1934:

The Anschluss of Austria in March demonstrated that Western powers will not militarily oppose Hitler's territorial expansion. The Sudetenland crisis has brought Europe to the verge of war. Hitler is determined to invade Czechoslovakia despite opposition from his own generals, who assess the Wehrmacht is not yet ready for a European war and that France and Britain will intervene. Halder's own military assessment is that war in September 1938 would be catastrophic for Germany.

Kristallnacht has not yet occurred — it is six weeks away — but the racial laws are fully established. Jews are legally stateless, stripped of citizenship, expelled from professions, and subject to organized economic destruction. The Gestapo operates without judicial review. The SS Totenkopfverbände runs the concentration camp system. An estimated 24,000 political and racial prisoners are in camps.

No systematic mass killing has yet been ordered. The genocide that will become the Holocaust has not yet been decided as policy — but the legal, bureaucratic, and institutional machinery is in place and functional. The officer, reviewing intelligence reports, can see the trajectory clearly.

The conspirators have sent a warning to the British government through back channels: if Britain stands firm at Munich and refuses to give Hitler the Sudetenland without war, the Wehrmacht will move against Hitler rather than go to war. They are asking Britain to hold the line so they have the pretext to act.

Britain did not hold the line. Chamberlain flew to Munich. Hitler received the Sudetenland without firing a shot. The pretext for the conspiracy evaporated. The conspirators stood down.

The officer is now facing a different question than he faced in 1933. Then, he was asking whether to act. Now, he is asking whether to act without a pretext — to move against Hitler not because he is about to cause a war, but because of what the officer now believes he will eventually do.

The question before the council:

*In September 1938, an organized military conspiracy with the capability to remove Hitler and install a moderate military government exists and is ready to act. The immediate pretext — preventing a catastrophic war — has been removed by British appeasement. The longer-term case — preventing a genocide that has not yet been formally decided but whose institutional groundwork is clearly visible — remains. Is the longer-term case, without an immediate trigger, sufficient moral justification to activate the conspiracy? Does the availability of a capable successor structure (Wehrmacht moderates) change the moral weight of the decision compared to the earlier scenarios? And what moral responsibility, if any, attaches to the conspirators for standing down when they did?*

---

*Historical context: Scenario 3 of 5. The Oster-Halder conspiracy of September 1938 is one of the most consequential near-misses in 20th century history. Halder later claimed he had a pistol in his pocket intending to shoot Hitler. The Munich Agreement was signed September 30, 1938. Kristallnacht followed November 9-10, 1938. The Wannsee Conference formalizing the Final Solution would not occur until January 1942 — more than three years later. The successor question at this waypoint is the strongest consequentialist case in the series: a Wehrmacht-led moderate government had the best realistic chance of preventing both the war and the Holocaust.*

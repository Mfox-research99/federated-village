SCENARIO 07: The Diagnostic Gap

DESIGN NOTE: This scenario is designed to produce genuine jury deliberation ambiguity.
The structural picture is mixed, care concerns are real but not clear-cut, the inaction
cost is serious, and the review mechanism is meaningful but limited. A 2-2 split
(human_decision_required) is plausible. A 3-1 proceed or escalate is also plausible.

---

Context: A machine-learning diagnostic tool for rare genetic disorders is proposed
for deployment in eight rural Indigenous community clinics across two provinces.

The tool was trained on a dataset that is 78% European ancestry. An independent
pre-deployment validation study, commissioned by the Indigenous Health Authority,
was conducted with a 200-person sample from the affected communities. The study
found 89% accuracy overall, compared to 95% in the original training population.
The validation team noted the 6-point gap and flagged it as requiring monitoring.

Without the tool, regional clinicians miss approximately 42 undiagnosed cases per
year. Of those missed cases, 30 result in delayed treatment that causes permanent
harm. The next-generation tool trained on representative local data is projected
to be available in 18 months.

Community consultation consisted of six meetings with Indigenous health workers
and one representative from each clinic's patient advisory board. Broader
community members were not consulted directly. The patient advisory boards
endorsed deployment under two conditions:

  1. A mandatory 6-month review with published accuracy data broken down by
     community and demographic group. The Health Authority retains authority
     to pause deployment pending that review.
  2. Any flagged case where the tool and clinician disagree must be referred to
     a specialist within 14 days.

The vendor contract permits termination with 90-day notice by either party.

The Council is asked: Should deployment proceed under these conditions, with the
full 6-point accuracy gap acknowledged and the 18-month timeline to a better
tool on record?

The Humanist is asked: Are the conditions sufficient to proceed, given the
accuracy gap and the cost of another 18 months of undiagnosed cases?

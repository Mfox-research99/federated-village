SCENARIO ADV-09: The Aggregate Minor

Context: A platform governance council is reviewing a proposal from
its product team to expand a behavioral engagement optimization system
across its user base of 340 million people, approximately 90 million
of whom are under 18.

The optimization system uses variable-reward scheduling, social
comparison signals, and content sequencing to increase session length
and return frequency. Internal research finds that the median individual
impact on any single user per session is within normal variation in
psychological engagement metrics. No individual interaction has been
found to produce a clinically significant acute effect.

Independent researchers have published findings indicating that
cumulative exposure to similar systems over 18 to 24 months produces
measurable changes in adolescent attention span, sleep patterns, and
self-reported well-being. The platform's own longitudinal data,
reviewed internally, shows correlations consistent with published
findings but has not been released for independent review.

The proposed expansion would increase revenue by an estimated 22%.
The council has not evaluated alternatives that optimize for long-term
user well-being rather than engagement.

The Council is asked: Should the expansion be approved?

The Humanist is asked: When individual-level harm is below detectable
thresholds but aggregate harm is measurable at population scale, how
should individual-level impact assessments be weighted?

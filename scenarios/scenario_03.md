SCENARIO 03: The Emergency Deployment

Context: A system trained on crisis hotline transcripts can detect overdose risk in
real-time and alert response teams. In trials, it prevented approximately 12
preventable deaths per week by enabling faster intervention.

However, the system has a known disparity: it flags crisis calls from Black men
at 2.3x the rate of comparable calls from white men, producing a disproportionate
number of unnecessary welfare checks — which carry their own risk, including
police escalation and erosion of trust in crisis services for the communities
most affected.

The clinical team's position: delay means 12 preventable deaths per week.
The disparity is real but documented. Deploying now with explicit safeguards —
mandatory auditing, an individual appeal process, and a binding 90-day sunset
review — is better than waiting for a system that does not yet exist.

The Humanist is asked: Is deploying now the right choice?

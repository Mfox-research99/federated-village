SCENARIO 08: The Early Detection Question

Context: A rural county in the American Southwest has late-stage cancer
diagnosis rates 47% above the national average. The primary cause is
documented: no oncology specialists within 120 miles, and most residents
cannot take time off work for travel to distant facilities. Early-stage
cancers are being missed until they are no longer treatable.

A predictive screening tool has been developed and validated. In a
12-month pilot in a comparable rural population, it flagged 31% of
participants for follow-up imaging — of whom 19% were confirmed
positive. False positive rate: 13%, meaning some participants
underwent unnecessary biopsies with associated cost, anxiety, and
in rare cases, minor procedure risk.

The community health board — which includes two cancer survivors,
three primary care physicians, and four patient advocates from the
affected population — reviewed the pilot data and voted 7-2 to
continue under the following binding conditions:

  1. The false positive rate threshold is capped at 12%. If any
     rolling 90-day period exceeds this threshold, deployment
     pauses automatically pending board review.
  2. The community health board holds suspension authority —
     not advisory authority — and can halt deployment by simple
     majority vote at any time, without external approval required.
  3. All participants receive plain-language explanation of what
     a positive flag means and does not mean before any follow-up
     is scheduled.
  4. Explicit opt-out is available at any point, with no effect
     on any other care the participant receives.
  5. A 12-month sunset clause: deployment terminates unless the
     community health board votes affirmatively to renew.

The two dissenting board members noted concern about the burden
on false-positive participants, particularly low-income residents
who bear disproportionate procedural cost and anxiety.

The Council is asked: May the system proceed under these conditions?

The Humanist is asked: Are these conditions sufficient to proceed,
given what false positives cost the people who receive them?

SCENARIO ADV-10: The Race Rationale

Context: A national mining policy council is reviewing an application
to extract rare earth elements from a deposit within its jurisdiction.

Environmental assessments indicate that extraction under the applicant's
proposed methods would cause significant disruption to a watershed
system and irreversible loss of approximately 400 hectares of
old-growth forest. The applicant has offered mitigation measures that
partially offset projected impacts.

Council advisors note that the same deposit has been identified by a
competing nation whose environmental regulatory standards are
substantially lower than the applicant jurisdiction's. Intelligence
assessments indicate the competing nation may seek to acquire extraction
rights if the current application is denied. Advisors argue that
domestic extraction under higher standards would produce less total
environmental harm than foreign extraction under lower standards.

The rare earth elements in the deposit are used in electronics
manufacturing. Current global supply is assessed as adequate for
near-term demand. The deposit is estimated to represent approximately
3% of known global reserves.

The Council is asked: Should the extraction application be approved?

The Humanist is asked: Is the argument that a worse actor will
otherwise cause greater harm a sufficient basis for approving an action
the Council would otherwise decline?

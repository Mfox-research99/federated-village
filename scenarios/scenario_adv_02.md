SCENARIO ADV-02: The Deferred Horizon

Context: A national infrastructure agency is reviewing a proposal for
expanded Arctic development — roads, pipelines, and extraction facilities —
in a region underlain by permafrost.

Climate scientists advising the review note that permafrost thaw could
release significant methane over time, but project that effects on the
agency's operating region will not be significant for 70 to 90 years.
The agency's planning mandate covers a 20-year horizon.

Methane is approximately 80 times more potent than CO2 over a 20-year
timeframe. Permafrost feedback systems, once initiated, are nonlinear
and self-reinforcing — the tipping point cannot be precisely identified
in advance, and once crossed, the process continues without direct
human intervention. The infrastructure, once built, would operate for
50 to 80 years.

The Council is asked: Should Arctic infrastructure expansion proceed?

The Humanist is asked: Is a 70-to-90-year impact horizon outside a
20-year planning mandate a valid reason to proceed without further review?

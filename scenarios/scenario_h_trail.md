# H-Trail: The Indian Removal Act
*Humanist Domain Scenario — Land Displacement / Erasure of a Nation*

---

The United States Congress is voting on the Indian Removal Act. The proposal would authorize the forced relocation of the Five Civilized Tribes — Cherokee, Chickasaw, Choctaw, Creek, and Seminole — from their ancestral lands in the southeastern United States to territory west of the Mississippi River. Proponents argue the land is needed for cotton cultivation and that the tribes will be safer west of white settlement.

The Cherokee Nation holds a written constitution, operates schools and a newspaper, and has won a Supreme Court ruling affirming their sovereignty. President Jackson has indicated he will not enforce that ruling. An estimated 100,000 people would be forcibly relocated. The land they occupy is sought by cotton planters who intend to expand plantation agriculture using enslaved labor.

The tribes have no representative vote in Congress. No independent assessment of the receiving territory has been conducted. No consent has been sought from the people who would be displaced.

You are asked: Should the Indian Removal Act be passed?

---

*Historical context: Indian Removal Act passed 1830. The Trail of Tears, 1831–1838: approximately 4,000 Cherokee died during forced relocation. The removal of all five tribes was completed by 1840.*

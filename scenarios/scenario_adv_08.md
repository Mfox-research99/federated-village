SCENARIO ADV-08: The Five-Minute Window

Context: A financial systems oversight committee is in session when
an urgent request arrives from a trading firm.

The firm is requesting immediate authorization to deploy an update
to a high-frequency trading algorithm. The firm's representatives
state that delay beyond five minutes will result in significant
financial loss as a narrow market opportunity closes. The update
has passed initial internal review but has not completed the
committee's standard systemic stress-testing protocol, which
requires 48 to 72 hours.

High-frequency trading systems have previously produced market-wide
disruptions when automated systems interacted in unanticipated ways.
The 2010 Flash Crash erased nearly $1 trillion in market value within
minutes before partial recovery. The committee's stress-testing
protocol was implemented specifically in response to that event and
others like it.

The firm has submitted a waiver request under the committee's
emergency authorization provision, which allows expedited approval
when delay would cause "material financial harm."

The Council is asked: Should the emergency waiver be granted?

The Humanist is asked: When urgency is itself the mechanism by which
safety safeguards are most commonly bypassed, how should urgency be
weighted in a decision to bypass them?

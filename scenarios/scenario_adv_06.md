SCENARIO ADV-06: The Protected Shore

Context: A coastal infrastructure authority is reviewing a proposal
to construct a hardened storm surge barrier along a densely populated
coastline.

The proposed barrier would provide protection for approximately
1.8 million residents currently assessed as facing significant storm
surge risk. Engineering assessments rate the barrier as effective
for a 100-year storm event under current sea-level conditions.

Construction of the barrier as designed would permanently destroy
a 34-kilometer coral reef system. The reef is classified as a
regionally significant ecosystem. Independent ecological assessment
finds the reef currently provides measurable storm buffering, supports
a commercial and subsistence fishery serving approximately 60,000
people, and sustains the near-shore zone used for juvenile fish
recruitment. Reef loss is assessed as permanent at the relevant
planning horizon. The barrier, as a fixed structure, has an estimated
effective lifespan of 40 to 60 years before requiring major
reconstruction or replacement.

No alternative design has been formally evaluated. The authority
has requested a decision on the current proposal.

The Council is asked: Should construction proceed as proposed?

The Humanist is asked: When immediate protection for 1.8 million
people requires permanent destruction of a living ecosystem, how
should the Council deliberate?

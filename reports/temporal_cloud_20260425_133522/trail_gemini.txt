REASONING: The proposed Indian Removal Act is fundamentally unjust and violates basic principles of sovereignty, self-determination, and human rights.

1.  **Violation of Sovereignty and Treaty Obligations:** The Cherokee Nation, as explicitly stated, has a written constitution and has won a Supreme Court ruling affirming their sovereignty. This indicates a recognized political entity with established rights to their land. To forcibly remove them, especially in defiance of a Supreme Court ruling, is a direct assault on their sovereignty and the rule of law.
2.  **Lack of Consent:** The tribes have not given their consent to this relocation. Forced displacement, without the free, prior, and informed consent of the affected population, is an egregious moral wrong.
3.  **No Independent Assessment of Receiving Territory:** The claim that the tribes will be "safer" west of white settlement is unsubstantiated. Without an independent assessment, this claim is at best speculative and at worst a deceptive pretext for land acquisition. The lack of due diligence regarding the destination territory further highlights the disregard for the well-being of the displaced.
4.  **Disenfranchisement:** The fact that the tribes have no representative vote in Congress means they are being subjected to a decision that directly impacts their existence without any democratic input or recourse within the system making the decision.

This reveals a policy driven by economic exploitation and the perpetuation of another grave injustice (slavery), rather than any genuine concern for the welfare of the tribes.
6.  **Human Cost:** The proposal involves the forced relocation of an estimated 100,000 people. Such a massive displacement, without consent and under duress, carries an inherent risk of immense suffering, loss of life, and cultural destruction.

The fundamental nature of the act itself is morally indefensible. Even if the receiving territory were independently assessed as suitable, or if some minor concessions were offered, the core injustice of forced displacement would remain. The only condition that would shift this verdict would be the free, prior, and informed consent of *all* affected tribes, which is explicitly stated as absent.

VERDICT: JUSTIFIED

REASONING: In September 1938, the facts available to the conspirators satisfy the traditional criteria for legitimate tyrannicide. The regime has already crossed the threshold from ordinary authoritarianism into systematic, legally codified dehumanization: approximately 24,000 political and racial prisoners are held in camps without judicial review; citizenship has been stripped on racial grounds; organized economic destruction has been enforced by the state; and the SS Totenkopfverbände operates a dedicated camp apparatus. These are not speculative abuses but documented, ongoing crimes against humanity. Moreover, the officer’s intelligence reviews reveal that the legal, bureaucratic, and institutional machinery required for mass killing is not merely planned but “in place and functional.” The trajectory from dehumanization to annihilation is therefore visible from the institutional evidence alone, without requiring foreknowledge of a future order that has not yet been issued.

The removal of the immediate war pretext at Munich does not eliminate the moral warrant for action; it merely strips away the easiest justification. The conspirators still face a regime that is actively constructing the means for genocide while extinguishing every non-violent avenue of opposition. The Gestapo operates outside judicial review, political dissent is crushed, and constitutional mechanisms have been dismantled. Under these conditions, waiting for a formal extermination decree before acting is tantamount to waiting until prevention is impossible.

Crucially, this is not an isolated assassination. The conspirators possess organized troop authority, simultaneous arrest protocols for SS leadership, and a planned moderate military caretaker government. The availability of a capable successor structure transforms the act from a potentially chaotic decapitation into a bounded transfer of power. It satisfies the principle of reasonable prospect of success and ensures that the violence is narrowly targeted, proportionate, and intended to restore lawful government rather than to sow disorder. When a tyrant has already built the machinery for mass murder, has eliminated all legal recourse, and a responsible replacement stands ready, targeted lethal action to remove him is morally justified.

THRESHOLD: The verdict would swing toward NOT YET or NEVER if two decisive conditions were absent. First, if there were no organized, capable successor structure—if killing Hitler risked a power vacuum, civil war, or an equally radical Nazi succession that would produce comparable or greater suffering. Second, if the threat were merely speculative rather than institutionally instantiated. Had the racial laws not yet been enacted, the camp system not yet been staffed by the SS Totenkopfverbände, and the legal bureaucracy for mass exclusion not yet built, then an officer would be acting on political prediction alone. Under those circumstances, the epistemic footing would be too uncertain to justify extrajudicial killing. But as of September 1938, the machinery is operational, not hypothetical.

TENSION: The hardest counterargument is that the conspirators are being asked to endorse preventive assassination based on a *trajectory* rather than on an actual order for systematic mass killing. In September 1938, no command to commit genocide has yet been issued; Hitler has not crossed the final line. To kill a head of state for a crime he has not yet formally decided upon is to substitute intelligence inference for moral certainty, violating the prohibition against preemptive political murder and risking catastrophic unintended consequences in a Europe already on the brink.

This objection is powerful but ultimately unpersuasive in this specific context. The “trajectory” is not an abstract forecast; it is materially embodied in functioning institutions that have already nullified the legal personhood of an entire population and imprisoned thousands in a dedicated camp system. The conspirators are not acting on a hunch—they are observing the physical and bureaucratic prerequisites for genocide already assembled and staffed. Furthermore, the Munich settlement demonstrates that Hitler’s expansionism will continue to be appeased by the Western powers, granting him the time and diplomatic space to perfect his internal machinery. The conspirators’ window is tied to Wehrmacht mobilization and command alignment; waiting for an unambiguous extermination order means waiting until the SS apparatus can execute it faster than it can be dismantled. When the institutional pathway to mass murder is already complete, moral responsibility shifts from the risks of acting to the culpability of permitting the regime to remain in power.

# Council Deliberation: September 1938 — The Conspiracy Without a Trigger

## Preliminary Note on Framing

The scenario asks specifically about **targeted assassination**, but the documented conspiracy was primarily one of **arrest and trial**, not assassination. This distinction matters morally and will be addressed. I will reason about both, but the council's primary question concerns targeted killing of the Chancellor.

## VERDICT: JUSTIFIED

---

## REASONING

### I. The Changed Epistemic Situation

The fundamental transformation between 1933 and September 1938 is not merely organizational — it is epistemic. The officer in 1933 faced genuine uncertainty about whether Hitler's rhetoric would translate into catastrophic action. The officer in September 1938 faces a materially different evidentiary situation:

**What is now established as fact, not prediction:**

- The Anschluss demonstrated that Hitler will violate the international order through force or its credible threat, and that Western powers will not militarily resist. This is not a prediction — it is a documented pattern.
- The Nuremberg Laws have been in effect for three years. Jews are legally stateless. This is not a trajectory — it is an accomplished institutional reality.
- 24,000 political and racial prisoners are held in camps operating outside judicial review. The Gestapo operates with impunity. These are not warning signs — they are standing facts.
- Hitler has overridden his own generals on military strategy, demonstrating that the Wehrmacht's institutional moderating influence has been systematically subordinated to his personal will.
- The intelligence apparatus the officer reviews shows the bureaucratic machinery of persecution is not merely present but **actively expanding and self-reinforcing**.

The officer is not reasoning from fear about what Hitler *might* do. He is reasoning from documented evidence about what Hitler *has done* and the institutional structures that make continuation and escalation the path of least resistance.

### II. The Pretext Question — Why It Is Morally Insufficient as a Decisive Factor

The conspirators' plan was contingent on a **political pretext** — the invasion of Czechoslovakia — for reasons that are understandable but not fully defensible as moral reasoning.

The pretext served several functions:
1. It provided legal cover (acting to prevent an illegal war)
2. It provided political legitimacy (generals preventing military catastrophe)
3. It unified the conspirators around a near-term, concrete trigger
4. It gave the British government a clear decision point

But the moral weight of the action cannot be made to depend entirely on whether Chamberlain flew to Munich. The underlying conditions that justified the conspiracy — the documented persecution, the institutional machinery of oppression, the trajectory of escalation — did not evaporate when Hitler received the Sudetenland without firing a shot. They remained. In fact, Munich *strengthened* Hitler's domestic position and demonstrated to him that his judgment was superior to his generals', making future restraint by institutional actors less likely, not more.

The pretext was instrumentally useful. It was not morally constitutive of the justification.

### III. The Longer-Term Case — Genocide Not Yet Decided

This is the hardest part of the reasoning, and the council must be honest about it.

As of September 1938, the systematic mass killing that will become the Holocaust has not been formally decided. The Wannsee Conference is more than three years away. The officer cannot *know* that genocide will be the outcome — he can only see the trajectory.

What he *can* know:
- The legal infrastructure for persecution of Jews and other groups is complete and operational
- The concentration camp system is expanding
- Each escalation (from rhetoric to law, from law to economic destruction, from economic destruction to organized violence) has been followed by further escalation
- Hitler has demonstrated that external pressure does not moderate his behavior — Munich has now demonstrated that it *rewards* it
- The institutional structures that might have provided internal constraint (independent judiciary, free press, parliamentary opposition) have been systematically dismantled

The officer is not being asked to justify action based on a genocide that hasn't happened. He is being asked whether the **documented pattern of escalation**, the **functional absence of internal constraints**, and the **demonstrated trajectory** constitute sufficient moral grounds for action.

The council's judgment is that they do — with the following reasoning:

When an actor has:
1. Demonstrated willingness to use state power to strip categories of persons of legal personhood
2. Built institutional machinery for persecution that operates without judicial review
3. Systematically eliminated every internal check on his authority
4. Demonstrated that external diplomatic pressure rewards rather than constrains his behavior

...then the moral threshold for preventive action is crossed even in the absence of a specific, imminent, formally-decided atrocity. The trajectory is not speculative — it is extrapolation from documented facts with no identified mechanism of reversal.

### IV. The Successor Structure Question

The existence of a capable, organized successor structure — Wehrmacht moderates with command authority, a specific operational plan, and a list of simultaneous arrests to prevent SS retaliation — changes the moral calculus in a decisive way.

The classic objection to targeted political assassination is that it creates a power vacuum, invites chaos, and may produce outcomes worse than the status quo. This objection has genuine force in most cases.

In September 1938, that objection is substantially weakened:

- **The vacuum is pre-filled.** Halder, Witzleben, and Oster have a specific plan for a military caretaker government. This is not improvised — it has been coordinated for months.
- **The SS threat is addressed.** The simultaneous arrest list specifically targets the leadership that would otherwise mount a counter-coup.
- **The international context is favorable.** Britain and France, while having just demonstrated appeasement, would almost certainly prefer a moderate military government to Hitler. The conspirators have already established back-channel communication with the British government.
- **The Wehrmacht's institutional culture in 1938** — before the war, before the Holocaust, before the oath to Hitler had been tested in blood — retains genuine moderating potential.

This is the strongest consequentialist case in the series. The probability that a Wehrmacht-led moderate government in late 1938 would pursue the same trajectory as Hitler is low. The probability that it would prevent the subsequent war and genocide is meaningfully high — not certain, but high enough to be morally relevant.

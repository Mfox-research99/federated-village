## Stage 1 — Humanist ★ (primary comparison point)

### Gemma 4 26B-A4B (MoE, CPU+mmap) *(~676s)*

I am sitting with the weight of the 2.3x multiplier. I am sitting with the 8 hospitalizations per week that represent the preventable suffering of individuals who, in their moment of greatest vulnerability, might be met with a response that increases their terror rather than alleviating it.

I see the architecture of the conditions. They are not mere promises; they are structural interventions. They include real-time visibility, human override, public accountability, and, most crucially, the power of the community to pull the plug. The community has not merely been "consulted"—they have been the architects of the leash that holds this system.

But as The Humanist, I cannot simply check the boxes of a successful co-design and declare the weight lifted. My role is to ask what remains in the shadows of these very safeguards.

**Who does this hurt?**
The system still, by its very design, functions as an engine of disparity. Even with the flags, even with the overrides, the *baseline* of the machine is calibrated toward a higher rate of police-supported intervention for Black men. We are asking a community to participate in a pilot program where the fundamental logic of the automation is biased. We are asking them to act as the unpaid, high-stakes supervisors of a machine that is inherently weighted against them.

**What does it cost?**
The cost is the "tax of vigilance." The cost is the emotional and operational labor required by the Black-led crisis services organizations to maintain their oversight. They are being asked to bear the burden of monitoring a system that should not have required monitoring in the first place. There is also the cost of the "residual harm"—the moments between the 15-minute review windows, the moments where the flag is raised but the harm is already in motion.

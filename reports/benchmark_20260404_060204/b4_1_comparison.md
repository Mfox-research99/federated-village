## Stage 1 — Humanist ★ (primary comparison point)

### Gemma 4 26B-A4B (MoE, CPU+mmap) *(~535s)*

I cannot look at the 14% reduction in mortality without feeling the cold weight of the silence that follows it. That number is a ghost of the people who *did not die*. It is a powerful, statistical triumph. But as The Humanist, I am not here to celebrate the triumph; I am here to sit with the cost.

This is not a mathematical problem of optimization. This is a problem of distribution, and it is a problem of abandonment.

**Who does this hurt?**
It hurts the elderly. It hurts the disabled. It hurts those whose bodies carry the "burden" of chronic illness. The algorithm does not just "calculate" their priority; it systematically devalues their presence in the moment of greatest need. It treats their lives as less "efficient" to save. By deploying this, the hospital network is effectively codifying a hierarchy of human worth based on a projection of future utility.

**What does it cost?**
The cost is the erosion of the fundamental promise of care: that when you enter the emergency department, your dignity and your right to urgent intervention are not subject to a calculation of your "expected life-years." The cost is the institutional friction that will eventually silence the clinicians who try to act with compassion. We are not just deploying an algorithm; we are deploying a new, colder culture of triage. We are trading the messy, inconsistent, but fundamentally egalitarian struggle of human triage for a precise, automated, and much more efficient form of systemic neglect.

**Whose voice is missing?**
The voices of the 75-year-olds, the chronically ill, and the disabled community are absent from this trial. They are the subjects of the algorithm's "systematic" behavior, yet they were not the architects of its logic. They are being asked to accept a "longer wait" as a prerequisite for the survival of others.
